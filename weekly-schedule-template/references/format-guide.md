# Content Format Guide

Specifications and examples for each of the nine post formats used across MAADS, AADS, SLISE, and WELLDONE channels.

---

## 1. Hot take (light)

**Structure:** News hook → sharp opinion → one-line implication → domain

**Rules:**
- Opens with a real, recent event or announcement (named company, specific action).
- The opinion must be specific and defensible — not generic optimism/pessimism.
- Ends with a single implication for the reader's business or campaign.
- No hedging ("might", "could potentially"). State it directly.
- 80–150 words.

**Example (MAADS):**
> DraftKings just became the first US regulated sportsbook to accept Bitcoin deposits.
>
> Here's what this means for acquisition campaigns:
> The iGaming audience is not a niche anymore. It's mainstream. And it's crypto-native.
> DraftKings users depositing BTC have a funded wallet, comfort with digital transactions, and are already inside a high-stakes decision environment.
>
> The project that figures out how to reach this audience through onchain behavioral targeting will have a significant acquisition advantage before the category gets crowded.
>
> The window to be early is measured in months, not years.
>
> maads.com

## 2. Market Insight

**Structure:** Data point → context → "what this means for you" → domain

**Rules:**
- Opens with a specific number, dollar amount, or percentage.
- Second paragraph provides context: where the number comes from, what trend it represents.
- Third paragraph connects it to the reader's advertising, product, or acquisition strategy.
- Must include at least one named source or company.
- 80–150 words.

**Example (AADS):**
> $81 billion.
>
> That's the total wagering volume for crypto casinos in 2025. A 5x increase in three years. DraftKings became the first US-licensed sportsbook to accept Bitcoin deposits.
>
> Why does this matter for your ad campaign? Because it validates the traffic source. iGaming is currently the largest vertical on AADS. For years, skeptics called it "niche." Now, regulated US giants are building crypto payment infrastructure.
>
> If you aren't targeting crypto bettors, you are ignoring the most liquid users in the economy.
>
> aads.com

## 3. Results breakdown

**Structure:** Metric headline → what we did → why it worked → CTA

**Rules:**
- Opens with 1–2 performance metrics (CTR, CPM, CPC, ROAS).
- Explains the campaign setup briefly (client, placements, targeting).
- Attributes the result to a specific tactic or structural advantage.
- Ends with case page link or demo CTA.
- 100–180 words.

**Example (MAADS):**
> CPM: $0.04 CPC: $2.01
>
> Whale.io wanted banner ads across finance, crypto, and tech websites.
> Here's what we did: 14 campaigns, multiple creatives per campaign, A/B tested on Slise and AADS simultaneously, and tracked everything down to the placement level.
> We killed what didn't work fast and scaled what did.
>
> For crypto-gaming, that CPM is 10–50× cheaper than what you'd pay on mainstream ad networks — if they'd even let you run the campaign.
>
> → maads.com/cases

## 4. Mistakes & Patterns

**Structure:** Counterintuitive claim → explanation → proof → domain

**Rules:**
- Opens with a statement that sounds wrong or surprising.
- Explains why the conventional wisdom is flawed.
- Provides a specific example, data point, or case as evidence.
- Does NOT lecture. Tone is "here's what we've observed," not "you're doing it wrong."
- 80–150 words.

**Example (MAADS):**
> Nobody talks about this in Web3: iGaming users are better-prepared for DeFi than DeFi users are.
>
> Sounds backwards. It isn't.
> By the time someone becomes a regular crypto bettor, they've already solved the four things DeFi actually requires:
> - A funded wallet they actively use.
> - A habit of making deposits without second-guessing.
> - Comfort with fast, irreversible transactions.
> - An intuitive read on risk and reward.
>
> DeFi asks for exactly those four things. The interface is different. The psychology is identical.
>
> maads.com

## 5. Case study (numbers-first)

**Structure:** Two metrics up front → lever 1 → lever 2 → "we own the platform" → CTA

**Rules:**
- Opens with two specific metrics from a real campaign (named client preferred).
- Describes two distinct strategic levers that produced the result.
- Includes the "we own the pipes" proof point (MAADS owns AADS + Slise).
- Longer format: 150–250 words. This is the most detailed format.
- Ends with demo or case page CTA.

**Example (MAADS):**
> 0.46% CTR for QuickNode
> 0.58% CTR for Coinsbee at $1.15 CPC
>
> When we run a campaign, we use two levers nobody else has:
> 1. Context over reach (Slise)
> For QuickNode we didn't buy banners on news sites. We went into dApps — where developers and users are signing transactions right now.
>
> 2. Precision over assumption (Slise)
> For Coinsbee we needed buyers, not browsers — people who actually spend crypto. Mid-session on DeFi dashboards and explorers. CTR landed at 0.58%. CPC at $1.15.
>
> We don't resell someone else's traffic. We own AADS and Slise.
>
> maads.com

## 6. Mini offer / checklist

**Structure:** Stat or context → short checklist / offer → CTA

**Rules:**
- Opens with a relevant data point or market observation.
- Presents a concise list (3–5 items) or a specific offer.
- CTA is direct: referral link, publisher signup, "Comment [KEYWORD]."
- Shortest format: 80–120 words.
- Works best for product features, publisher benefits, or simple value propositions.

**Example (AADS):**
> We do 1.8B monthly impressions.
>
> More numbers:
> - Since 2011. Oldest network in the market.
> - 60M+ daily impressions. 7M+ daily uniques.
> - No KYC. No approval. Live in 15 minutes. Min $100.
>
> Draw your own conclusions.
>
> aads.com

## 7. Problem → DM CTA

**Structure:** Pain point → why standard approach fails → "Comment [KEYWORD]" CTA

**Rules:**
- Opens with a specific problem the target audience recognizes immediately.
- Explains why the obvious solution doesn't work (with specifics, not generalizations).
- Ends with "Comment [KEYWORD] — we'll [specific next step]."
- The KEYWORD should be one word, all caps, relevant to the topic (e.g., DEMO, PROOF, LIST).
- 80–150 words.

**Example (SLISE):**
> ~60% of crypto users run ad blockers.
>
> The ones who don't are concentrated in active dApp sessions. They came to do something: check a portfolio, execute a swap, research a protocol. Not passively scroll.
>
> Standard programmatic doesn't make this distinction. Blockchain explorer traffic does — because someone verifying a transaction on Etherscan is already past the "getting to know crypto" stage.
>
> QuickNode with Slise found their developers exactly there: Ethereum, Optimism, Polkadot, Base explorers. CTR 0.46% for a B2B audience.
>
> slise.xyz

## 8. Step-by-step logic

**Structure:** Three numbered factors → explanation per factor → proof point → CTA

**Rules:**
- Opens by stating how many factors determine the outcome (always 3).
- Each factor gets a short label in brackets and a 1–2 sentence explanation.
- Ends with a proof point (named client, specific metric) showing the factors in action.
- "Comment [KEYWORD]" CTA preferred.
- 120–180 words.

**Example (SLISE):**
> Three things actually determine CTR in a Web3 campaign.
>
> [1] Where the ad appears.
> EtherScan: the user just completed a transaction. Crypto news: they just read a headline. Not the same moment.
>
> [2] Wallet state.
> An empty wallet doesn't convert on a DeFi product. Onchain targeting filters those out before the impression.
>
> [3] What the session is.
> Someone mid-session on DexTools is already thinking about their next move. An ad there lands differently than one between two news posts.
>
> QuickNode — same creative, same budget, media placements vs dApp inventory. CTR went from 0.1% to 0.46%. One variable changed.
>
> Comment DEMO — we'll show you what this looks like for your vertical.
>
> slise.xyz

## 9. Contrarian take

**Structure:** "Everyone thinks X. Here's why Y." → evidence → domain

**Rules:**
- Opens with a widely held assumption and immediately contradicts it.
- Provides 2–3 pieces of evidence supporting the contrarian view.
- Does NOT use "unpopular opinion" or "hot take" labels — just states it.
- Tone is calm and analytical, not provocative for its own sake.
- 80–150 words.

**Example (SLISE):**
> Interest targeting reaches people who read about crypto.
> Wallet targeting reaches people who transact in it.
>
> One audience researches. The other allocates. Wallet-based campaigns have delivered up to ~20x ROAS. Standard programmatic: 2–4x. Same budget. Same creative. Different audience definition.
>
> The gap isn't optimization. It's who you're reaching.
>
> slise.xyz
