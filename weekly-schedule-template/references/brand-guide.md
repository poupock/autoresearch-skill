# Brand Guide

Voice, topics, and CTA patterns for each brand.

---

## MAADS (maads.com)

**What it is:** Full-service crypto marketing agency. Owns and operates AADS and Slise. Positions as the only agency that controls both the ad network and the targeting layer.

**Voice:** Strategic, confident, data-backed. Speaks from the position of someone who sees the entire market — advertiser side, publisher side, and the tech layer in between. Uses "we" when referencing platform ownership. Never salesy or hype-driven.

**Core topics:**
- Crypto marketing strategy and market trends
- iGaming ↔ DeFi audience crossover
- Campaign performance data (CTR, CPM, CPC, ROAS)
- Platform comparisons (AADS vs Google/Meta restrictions)
- Macro events affecting crypto ad spend (Fed decisions, regulation, market cycles)
- World Cup / major sporting events as iGaming acquisition windows
- AI/GEO trends in marketing
- Agentic commerce and AI-driven discovery

**CTA patterns:**
- `maads.com` (soft — most common)
- `maads.com/cases` (case study page)
- `maads.com/cases/[client]` (specific case)
- "Book a demo" / "Comment PROOF"
- "Comment DEMO — we'll walk you through [specific thing]"

**Formats used:** Hot take, Market Insight, Mistakes & Patterns, Case study (numbers-first), Results breakdown, Contrarian take

**Do NOT:**
- Position MAADS as "just an agency" — it's an agency that owns its own ad infrastructure
- Use generic marketing speak ("ROI-driven solutions", "cutting-edge")
- Claim results without naming the client or citing a real metric

## AADS (aads.com)

**What it is:** Crypto ad network operating since 2011. 300M+ daily impressions across 2,500+ publisher sites. No KYC required. Minimum budget $100. Supports BTC, ETH, USDT, Lightning, Visa/MC, and 16+ other payment methods. Self-serve platform — campaigns go live in minutes.

**Voice:** Direct, no-nonsense, product-focused. Lets the numbers speak. Comfortable being blunt about what it is and what it isn't. Oldest player in the market — uses history as credibility without being nostalgic.

**Core topics:**
- Network stats (impressions, uniques, publisher count)
- CPM ranges and pricing model ($0.04–$2.40 range)
- Payment methods (crypto + fiat, Lightning settlement)
- Platform restrictions on competitors (Google, Meta, ChatGPT banning crypto)
- Publisher benefits (auction model, no empty banners, one-line embed)
- iGaming as largest vertical
- Referral program (10% for 6 months)
- Bear market = cheap CPM advantage
- Creative format tips (GIF/WebM > static)

**CTA patterns:**
- `aads.com` (most common)
- `aads.com/referral` (referral program)
- `aads.com/blog/[slug]` (blog posts / comparisons)
- "Publishers: one HTML embed. That's it."

**Formats used:** Market Insight, Mini offer / checklist, Problem → DM CTA, Step-by-step logic, Contrarian take

**Do NOT:**
- Oversell targeting sophistication — AADS is a volume network, Slise is the precision layer
- Downplay competitors dishonestly — compare on facts (pricing, access, restrictions)
- Use "we" to mean MAADS when speaking as AADS

## SLISE (slise.xyz)

**What it is:** On-chain ad targeting network that places banners directly inside dApps — Etherscan, DexTools, BirdEye, Solscan, GeckoTerminal, DexScreener, Poocoin, and others. Targets based on wallet state, chain, and session context rather than cookies or interest data.

**Voice:** Technical, precise, evidence-based. Speaks to media buyers and growth leads who understand CTR benchmarks. Uses specific publisher names and chain names. More reserved than MAADS — lets the targeting methodology do the talking.

**Core topics:**
- dApp-native ad placements (specific publisher names)
- Wallet-state targeting vs interest targeting
- CTR benchmarks (0.4–0.6% vs 0.1% industry average)
- Named case results (QuickNode 0.46%, Coinsbee 0.58%)
- Chain-specific audiences (Solana vs Ethereum vs Base)
- Session context (mid-transaction vs passive browsing)
- Etherscan session duration (9min 51sec avg)
- Ad blocker penetration (~60%) and why dApp sessions bypass it
- Daily active wallet counts (24.3M dApp ecosystem)

**CTA patterns:**
- `slise.xyz` (most common)
- "Comment LIST — we'll send the full publisher list"
- "Comment DEMO — we'll show you what this looks like for your vertical"

**Formats used:** Results breakdown, Market Insight, Hot take, Step-by-step logic, Problem → DM CTA, Contrarian take, Mini offer / checklist

**Do NOT:**
- Claim Slise works for broad awareness campaigns — it's a precision tool
- Confuse Slise publishers with AADS publishers — different inventory
- Use "on-chain targeting" without explaining what it means in practice

## WELLDONE

**What it is:** Engineering team of 30+ senior developers. Provides full-ownership development for FinTech, Web3, and AdTech products. Time & Materials only — no fixed scope, no vendor lock-in. Client owns all IP, docs, and source code from day one. Walk-away monthly.

**Voice:** Calm, technical, understated. Doesn't oversell. States what it does and lets the model speak for itself. More "engineering blog" than "agency pitch." Comfortable discussing AI in engineering honestly — acknowledges benefits without hype.

**Core topics:**
- Full code ownership model (IP, docs, source from day one)
- CX Engineering (CX embedded in every sprint, not a separate phase)
- One blended rate for entire team (engineers, designers, QA, PM)
- AI in engineering (41% AI-generated code, security implications)
- EU AI Act compliance (August 2, 2026 deadline, penalties)
- FinTech compliance and security-first development
- Production systems already running (AADS, Slise, e-commerce)
- Monthly flexibility (rearrange User Stories, no change-request fees)

**CTA patterns:**
- No domain CTA yet — posts end with the value statement
- Occasional soft CTA referencing production systems as proof

**Formats used:** Results breakdown, Hot take (light)

**Do NOT:**
- Promise specific timelines or cost savings
- Use "move fast and break things" language — WELLDONE's pitch is the opposite
- Claim AI replaces engineers — the message is "AI amplifies senior engineers who own decisions"
- Market WELLDONE the same way as MAADS/AADS — completely different audience (CTOs, VPs of Engineering, founders evaluating dev partners)
